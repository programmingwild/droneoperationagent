# FAA Part 107 — Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems (condensed for RAG)

Condensed from the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 14 CFR Part 107. This is a training
corpus summary, not legal advice. Always consult the current eCFR.

## § 107.3 Definitions
Small unmanned aircraft: < 55 lb on takeoff including everything attached. Small UAS = the aircraft
plus communication links and control components. Visual observer (VO): a designated person assisting
the remote PIC to see and avoid other air traffic or objects aloft.

## § 107.12 Remote pilot certificate
No person may manipulate the flight controls or act as remote pilot in command (rPIC) unless that
person holds a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating and satisfies § 107.65 recency.

## § 107.15 Condition for safe operation
Prior to each flight, the remote PIC must check the small UAS to determine it is in a condition for
safe operation. No person may continue flight when the system is no longer in a condition for safe
operation.

## § 107.21 In-flight emergency
In an in-flight emergency requiring immediate action, the rPIC may deviate from any rule of this part
to the extent necessary to meet that emergency. Each deviation must be reported to the Administrator
upon request.

## § 107.29 Operation at night
Night operations require (1) an initial knowledge test or training completed after April 6, 2021, and
(2) lighted anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles with a flash rate sufficient
to avoid collision. During civil twilight, anti-collision lighting is also required.

## § 107.31 Visual line of sight (VLOS)
The rPIC, VO (if used), and person manipulating the controls must be able to see the unmanned
aircraft throughout the entire flight without aided vision other than corrective lenses, to know its
location, determine attitude/altitude/direction, observe airspace, and determine it does not endanger
others.

## § 107.33 Visual observers
Effective communication between rPIC, VO, and person manipulating controls must be maintained at all
times; the VO must be able to see the aircraft as in § 107.31; they must coordinate to scan airspace
and maintain awareness.

## § 107.35 Multiple aircraft
A person may not manipulate flight controls or act as rPIC or VO for more than one unmanned aircraft
at the same time.

## § 107.37 Right of way
Small unmanned aircraft must yield the right of way to all aircraft, airborne vehicles, and launch
and reentry vehicles. They may not pass over, under, or ahead of another aircraft unless well clear.

## § 107.39 Operation over human beings
No operation over a human being unless that person is directly participating, is under a covered
structure or inside a stationary vehicle, or the operation meets an operational category in Subpart D
(Categories 1–4).

## § 107.41 Operation in certain airspace
No person may operate in Class B, C, or D airspace, or within the lateral boundaries of the surface
area of Class E airspace designated for an airport, unless prior authorization from Air Traffic
Control (ATC) is obtained. Authorizations are commonly issued via LAANC.

## § 107.43 Vicinity of airports
No person may operate in a manner that interferes with operations and traffic patterns at any airport,
heliport, or seaplane base.

## § 107.45 Prohibited or restricted areas
No operation in prohibited or restricted areas without permission from the using or controlling agency.

## § 107.47 Flight restrictions near TFRs
The rPIC must comply with §§ 91.137–91.145 and 99.7 (flight restrictions in proximity to certain
areas designated by notice to airmen — e.g., disaster TFRs, stadium TFRs, special security TFRs).

## § 107.49 Preflight familiarization and inspection
Before flight the rPIC must: (a) assess the operating environment — local weather, local airspace and
flight restrictions, location of persons and property, other ground hazards; (b) brief all direct
participants on operating conditions, emergency and contingency procedures, roles/responsibilities and
hazards; (c) verify control links; (d) ensure enough power for the intended operation time; (e) secure
any attached objects; (f) for operations over human beings, confirm the aircraft meets the applicable
category requirements.

## § 107.51 Operating limitations
(a) Groundspeed ≤ 87 knots (100 mph). (b) Altitude ≤ 400 ft AGL unless flown within a 400-ft radius of
a structure and no higher than 400 ft above the structure's uppermost limit. (c) Flight visibility ≥
3 statute miles from the control station. (d) Minimum cloud clearance: 500 ft below the cloud and
2,000 ft horizontally from the cloud.

## § 107.65 Aeronautical knowledge recency
A person may not exercise rPIC privileges unless, within the previous 24 calendar months, they passed
an initial aeronautical knowledge test, completed recurrent training, or (for pilot certificate
holders) completed § 107.74 training.

## § 107.200/205 Waivers
FAA may waive several operating rules (e.g., night, VLOS, over-people, operating limitations) via
certificate of waiver under § 107.200; § 107.205 lists rules subject to waiver.
