# DGCA Drone Rules 2021 — India (condensed for RAG)

Condensed summary of India's Drone Rules, 2021 and Digital Sky guidance. Training corpus, not legal
advice. Consult the DGCA and the Digital Sky Platform before flight.

## Applicability and categories
Applies to civil unmanned aircraft systems operating in Indian airspace. Weight categories:
Nano (≤ 250 g), Micro (250 g – 2 kg), Small (2–25 kg), Medium (25–150 kg), Large (> 150 kg).
Nano drones used for non-commercial purposes are exempt from most requirements; all other categories
and all commercial use are in full scope of the rules.

## Unique Identification Number (UIN)
All drones other than nano drones flown for non-commercial purposes must be registered and carry a
Unique Identification Number (UIN) issued via the Digital Sky Platform. Nano drones used for R&D are
exempt from UIN.

## Remote pilot certificate
Required for small, medium and large category drones, and for commercial operations of micro drones.
Obtained by completing training at an authorised remote pilot training organisation (RPTO) and passing
DGCA tests. Validity and any limitations are governed by DGCA.

## Airspace zones (Digital Sky)
- Green zone: airspace where drone operations up to 400 ft (120 m) AGL do not require prior
  permission, subject to Digital Sky zone conditions.
- Yellow zone: controlled airspace — prior airspace authorisation (ATC clearance) required before
  operation.
- Red zone: prohibited — operation only with prior permission of the central government.

## Altitude limit
Maximum 120 m (400 ft) above ground level for all categories. Additional height restrictions apply
near aerodromes and in controlled airspace.

## NPNT (No Permission – No Takeoff)
Drones above the nano category must comply with NPNT: the flight planning application requests
permission from the Digital Sky system before takeoff, and the drone will not arm without an approved
permission artifact.

## Insurance and operator obligations
Third-party liability insurance is required for non-nano drones. Operators must log every flight.
Serious safety incidents must be reported to DGCA. Import of drones is separately regulated by DGFT.

## Equipment and operational requirements
- Nano: reduced requirements; still cannot be flown in a manner that endangers persons or property.
- All other categories: registered UIN, NPNT compliance, insurance, remote pilot certificate.
- Carriage of dangerous goods is prohibited. Drones must not drop articles without permission.

## Pre-flight requirements (DGCA)
Before each flight: verify the zone and obtain any required authorisation via Digital Sky, check
equipment and airworthiness, verify weather, brief everyone involved, and log the flight after
completion.
